# STYLE Global Export Expansion 2026

## Intensive Research Study — World 50 + Europe 50 + Campaign Case Study

### Executive Summary
This study is a second-generation expansion model built after STYLE's first large outbound test. It is intentionally separate from the older 100-country radar.

The first campaign contacted more than 5,000 business emails across 50 countries while promoting five core STYLE products:
1. Sunny Minya
2. Galala
3. Meli Grey
4. New Halayeb Granite
5. Ramady El Sherka Granite

Observed campaign result supplied by the project owner:
- 50 markets contacted
- 5,000+ emails sent
- 10 positive commercial replies
- 1 near-order / specification-stage opportunity
- 9 future-potential accounts

Using 5,000 only as the minimum denominator, the positive-commercial-conversation rate was at most 0.20%. The near-order signal rate was below 0.02%. These are observed pilot outcomes, not future forecasts.

The core conclusion is that STYLE no longer needs wider country discovery. It needs higher buyer precision: recent shipment activity + exact product fit + decision-maker access + country-specific offer + fast RFQ conversion.

## 3. New Priority Model

The new STYLE Priority Score is not a probability of purchase.

Weights:
- Recent shipment / buyer activity: 30%
- Product fit with STYLE five: 20%
- Egypt-origin compatibility: 15%
- Import-demand scale: 15%
- Decision-maker / public-data accessibility: 8%
- Recent RFQ / buying signal: 5%
- Logistics / market access: 4%
- Commercial / compliance risk: 3%

This structure deliberately gives more weight to real recent buyer activity than the older model.

## 6. Campaign Case Study

The first large campaign proved one important fact: volume alone is not enough.

5,000+ emails across 50 markets generated:
- 10 positive commercial conversations
- 1 near-order / specification-stage opportunity
- 9 future-potential relationships

The test does not show which country performed best because exact sent, delivered, reply and RFQ counts per country were not provided.

Possible friction points:
1. company lists may have included weak-fit businesses
2. email may have gone to generic inboxes instead of purchasing/import decision makers
3. the same product/message mix may have been used across different market structures
4. recent import activity was not always a required filter
5. five products were shown, but product-market relevance may not have been personalized
6. deliverability quality is unknown without delivered/bounce/spam data
7. website and technical proof may affect conversion after the first click

Strategic shift:
Old: 5,000 broad emails → wait for replies
New: shipment-verified account → product match → decision maker → personalized first touch → RFQ route → 72-hour commercial response

## 7. Golden Account Definition

A Golden Account should satisfy most of:
- imported marble/granite/slabs within last 3–12 months
- relevant HS/product description
- repeat shipment pattern
- business model fits importer/distributor/fabricator/project supplier
- active website and real commercial presence
- identifiable procurement/import/owner contacts
- product mix overlaps STYLE five
- acceptable country/commercial risk

Recommended account score:
- Recent shipment activity: 30
- STYLE product match: 20
- repeat frequency: 10
- company scale: 10
- Egypt-origin compatibility: 10
- decision-maker access: 8
- recent RFQ/buying signal: 7
- email/contact quality: 5

90+ = Golden
80–89 = A-Class
65–79 = Qualified/Nurture
Below 65 = do not prioritize

## 8. Buyer Segments

Importer / Distributor: highest priority for recurring container supply. Offer: trial container → stock programme → annual volume agreement.

Stone Yard / Wholesaler: repeat availability, sizes, landed economics, stock replenishment.

Fabricator / Processor: slabs/blocks, yield, consistency, batch quality, repeat processor supply.

Contractor / Project Supplier: BOQ, cut-to-size, finish, packing, lead time, phased delivery.

Monument / Headstone: granite durability, standard dimensions, technical consistency.

Architect / Specifier: sample/specification pack, technical documentation and material identity.

## 9. Market-Specific Messaging

GCC: project reliability, available formats, capacity, lead time and logistics.

France / Germany / Northern Europe: technical proof, consistency, origin documentation, repeat supply and differentiated Egyptian stone.

USA / Canada / Australia: distributor economics, unique Egyptian products, stock programme and recurring container supply.

## 10. SWOT 2026

Strengths:
- Egyptian-origin portfolio across beige, grey marble and granite
- export-ready material formats
- strategic location for GCC, Europe and Africa
- documented Egyptian-origin buying in important markets
- five-product campaign architecture supports focused selling

Weaknesses:
- current observed positive-reply rate from mass campaign is low
- missing country-level campaign analytics
- decision-maker quality and shipment recency need stronger controls
- technical/website proof consistency can reduce conversion
- broad email volume may harm deliverability

Opportunities:
- daily-refreshed shipment databases allow stronger account qualification
- active RFQs indicate current demand
- Egypt-origin success can be used as a ranking multiplier
- multiple campaigns can be segmented by importer, processor, project and monument buyer
- France/Germany/Romania/UAE/Saudi/Morocco have particularly strong data depth

Threats:
- Turkey, Italy, India, Brazil, China, Spain and Portugal have strong competitive positions
- public trade databases can produce false positives
- price-led selling can commoditize STYLE
- sanctions/payment/compliance risks in selected markets
- competitors can reach the same shipment-verified buyers

## 11. 72-Hour RFQ Conversion System

0–2 hours: acknowledge request; confirm material, size, thickness, finish, quantity and destination; assign owner.

2–8 hours: current batch photos/video; available sizes; packing; MOQ; production/availability window; preliminary FOB.

8–24 hours: full quotation; CIF option where feasible; technical sheet; sample offer; payment terms subject to company policy.

24–72 hours: follow-up call/WhatsApp if publicly provided or consented; answer objections; confirm next-step date; convert to sample/PO/meeting.
